# The Version Where You Left Me

## Loop One: The Rooms I Kept Ready

### Denial

**Maze Memories**

1. You had always been quiet. I mistook the new silence for more of the same.
2. I knocked before entering, then opened the door before you answered.
3. You stopped leaving your sketchbook downstairs because I asked what everything meant.
4. I told myself every child kept secrets from the person who knew them best.
5. At dinner, you said nothing was wrong, and I accepted the words instead of your face.

**Room Closing Memory**

I found you sitting on the floor between the bed and the wall, still wearing your coat. When I asked what had happened, you said, "I don't know how to tell you things." I laughed softly and said, "You can tell me anything." Then I spent ten minutes explaining why.

### Anger

**Maze Memories**

1. You cut your hair without warning, as though your own face concerned only you.
2. I called your closed door disrespectful and my raised voice concern.
3. Your friends knew opinions you had never brought home.
4. I said the house had rules when what I meant was that I should not be surprised.
5. Every choice you made alone felt like a choice made against me.

**Room Closing Memory**

You came home wearing a jacket I had never seen. I asked who bought it, where you had been, why your phone was off, and what else you were hiding. You stood beneath the hall light and said, "It's just a jacket." I remember how furious I was that something could be just yours.

### Bargaining

**Maze Memories**

1. I promised more freedom after your grades improved, then worried freedom would ruin them.
2. You could go if I drove you, stayed nearby, and knew when you were leaving.
3. I said trust had to be earned whenever you asked me to give some first.
4. We made agreements whose conditions changed as soon as you met them.
5. I offered choices with one answer that would not hurt me.

**Room Closing Memory**

We sat at the kitchen table and wrote your new rules together. You chose the pen. I chose the curfew, the places, the names I needed, and what would happen if you were late. At the bottom, I made us both sign. You stared at my name beside yours and asked, "Which part did I choose?"

### Depression

**Maze Memories**

1. You learned how to cross the whole house without beginning a conversation.
2. I heard you laugh through the wall and could not remember the last time you had laughed with me.
3. Dinner became the sound of cutlery proving we were still a family.
4. Your answers grew smaller until yes and no were too revealing.
5. I missed you most when you were one room away.

**Room Closing Memory**

I made the meal you used to request on birthdays. You thanked me, ate three bites, and carried your plate to the sink. I remained at the table after the food went cold, offended by the empty chair and unable to admit how long you had been leaving it.

### Acceptance

**Maze Memories**

1. I helped you compare schools as long as none were too far away.
2. You packed books I had forgotten belonged to you.
3. I folded your clothes, and you unfolded them into different boxes.
4. I said your room would remain exactly as you left it.
5. On your final morning, I let you carry your own key.

**Room Closing Memory**

At the station, I kept finding reasons to straighten your collar. When the announcement came, you held me carefully, as if sudden movement might make the moment worse. I told you home would always be waiting. You said, "I know." I heard a promise in it because I needed one.

## Loop Two: The Distance Between Replies

### Denial

**Maze Memories**

1. Your calls became shorter, but your work had become demanding.
2. I left messages saying there was no need to call back, then counted the hours.
3. You visited twice that year; I described it to others as often.
4. I kept buying the tea you liked in case you came without telling me.
5. When you said you needed space, I cleaned your room to make more.

**Room Closing Memory**

Your birthday fell on a Tuesday. I set the table for two, though we had made no plans. At nine, you sent a message: "Thank you for thinking of me." I read it until the sentence sounded affectionate enough.

### Anger

**Maze Memories**

1. You had acquired words like boundaries from people who did not know us.
2. I blamed the city for teaching you to call distance healthy.
3. You said my questions felt like inspections; I said guilt made people defensive.
4. I told relatives only my side because yours was private.
5. The less you answered, the more urgently I deserved an answer.

**Room Closing Memory**

You asked me not to discuss you with the family. I waited until we hung up, then called your aunt and repeated the request word for word. I needed someone to confirm how cruel it sounded. She was quiet for a long time before saying, "They asked you not to do exactly this."

### Bargaining

**Maze Memories**

1. I apologized for upsetting you and waited for you to apologize for being upset.
2. Every gift carried a small question about whether I was forgiven yet.
3. I offered to pay for the journey if the destination was home.
4. I promised not to mention the past after explaining it one final time.
5. I said we could begin again as long as we began with my intentions.

**Room Closing Memory**

I wrote, "I'm sorry for whatever you think I did." The sentence looked generous on the screen. Beneath it, I added six paragraphs about everything I had sacrificed. You replied with one line: "This is what I mean." I searched the message for an accusation I could answer.

### Depression

**Maze Memories**

1. The first unanswered month was loud; the sixth was ordinary.
2. Your room remained ready for a person who no longer needed it.
3. I stopped telling people you were busy and began saying we were complicated.
4. Holidays passed without arguments because they passed without you.
5. I knew your address and understood that knowing it was not an invitation.

**Room Closing Memory**

One winter morning, a pipe burst above your room. Water stained the ceiling and curled the photographs on the shelf. I stood with a bucket in my hands, watching the room change without your permission, and realized I had been preserving it for myself.

### Acceptance

**Maze Memories**

1. I stopped calling from unfamiliar numbers when you did not answer mine.
2. I put the spare key in a drawer instead of beneath the mat.
3. Your birthday passed with one unsent message.
4. I learned that waiting could also be a way of refusing to move.
5. I said, "They don't speak to me," without adding what I had done to deserve better.

**Room Closing Memory**

I took your cup from the cupboard and used it for my own tea. Nothing broke. You did not vanish more completely. It was only a cup, and the morning was only a morning, and for once I let both things remain small.

## Loop Three: What the Door Remembered

### Denial

**Maze Memories**

1. The recordings were old; people sounded harsher on old machines.
2. I remembered asking what you wanted, even when the tape did not.
3. A camera captured minutes, not the years of care surrounding them.
4. I said your silence had always been natural until I heard myself interrupt it.
5. Memory had protected me so faithfully that truth felt like an intruder.

**Room Closing Memory**

On the recording, someone asked what you wanted to be. You inhaled to answer. My voice arrived first: "They've always been practical." Everyone laughed, including you. I replayed that small laugh until I could hear how carefully you had placed it.

### Anger

**Maze Memories**

1. I resented the younger version of me for leaving evidence.
2. I wanted one memory in which your distance was entirely your fault.
3. Love should have counted for more than the shape I forced it to take.
4. I became furious each time the truth refused to make me innocent.
5. It felt unfair that understanding the harm did not undo it.

**Room Closing Memory**

I threw the old rule sheet away, then pulled it from the bin and smoothed it flat. Your signature was smaller than mine. For years I had remembered us agreeing. The paper remembered two people at a table and only one of them being allowed to decide.

### Bargaining

**Maze Memories**

1. I wrote an apology whose final sentence asked you to come home.
2. I removed the excuses but kept checking whether enough remained to forgive.
3. I imagined the exact words that would purchase one reply.
4. I promised to expect nothing while rehearsing our reunion.
5. Even remorse became another door I wanted you to open for me.

**Room Closing Memory**

The letter began with "I understand now" and ended with "Please answer." I read those lines together until I saw the bargain between them. Then I started again with what I had done, not what I had meant, and left the final page empty of instructions.

### Depression

**Maze Memories**

1. Some apologies arrive after the person who needed them has stopped waiting.
2. I could change and still never be known as changed by you.
3. There was no honest version in which I had loved you perfectly.
4. I mourned the years you had spent learning to disappear in front of me.
5. The hardest consequence was one I could not ask you to relieve.

**Room Closing Memory**

I sat on the floor beside the box of recordings and finally pictured your life without placing myself outside its door. Perhaps you were happy. Perhaps you still checked every sentence for hidden conditions. I had no right to ask which.

### Acceptance

**Maze Memories**

1. You left, and I had been leaving you first in smaller ways.
2. My love was real; so was the harm I taught it to excuse.
3. An apology could be true without becoming a summons.
4. I stopped using your absence as proof that I had done nothing wrong.
5. The first boundary I fully respected was the one that kept me outside.

**Room Closing Memory**

I sealed the letter and wrote your name, but not your address. You had asked me not to contact you. For once, I did not treat your answer as the beginning of a negotiation. I placed the envelope in the drawer beside the spare key and let that be the only reply I was owed.

**Reveal**

I loved you by preparing the world before you reached it. I chose the safe road, the sensible clothes, the friends I trusted, the future I could understand. Whenever you resisted, I mistook fear for wisdom and control for care. You became quiet. I called you private. You learned to keep your life in places my questions could not reach. I called that secrecy. When you finally left home, I told myself the distance had begun at the station.

You tried to tell me. You shortened calls, then explained why. You asked me not to repeat your life to relatives, and I repeated the request while asking them to take my side. You asked for space, and I filled it with messages, gifts, apologies, and reasons. When you stopped answering, I told everyone you had abandoned me. It was the only version in which all my love made me innocent.

Years later, I heard my voice on an old recording. You were asked a question, and I answered before you could. It was such a small thing. That was what made it impossible to dismiss. There had been no single cruelty for me to confess, only a thousand moments in which being your parent meant never allowing you to be beyond me.

I wrote the apology at last. The first version asked you to return. The second asked you to forgive me. The final version asked nothing. I did not send it. You had already given me one clear request, and respecting it was the first change I could not make you witness. You were the one who walked through the door. I was the one who taught you that leaving was the only answer I would hear.
